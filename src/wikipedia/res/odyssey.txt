'''''The Odyssey''''' is a 2026 epic<!--DO NOT LINK OR PIPE as this is an OVERLINK--> [[action fantasy film]]<!--Do not add other genres, this is described as a "mythic action epic".--> written and directed by [[Christopher Nolan]], who produced it with his wife [[Emma Thomas]]. An adaptation of [[Homer]]'s [[ancient Greek]] epic poem the ''[[Odyssey]]'', it stars an [[ensemble cast]] including [[Matt Damon]], [[Tom Holland]], [[Anne Hathaway]], [[Robert Pattinson]], [[Lupita Nyong'o]], [[Samantha Morton]], [[Zendaya]], and [[Charlize Theron]]. In the film, [[Odysseus]] (Damon), the Greek king of [[Homer's Ithaca|Ithaca]], undergoes a long and perilous journey home after the [[Trojan War]] and encounters mythical beings as he attempts to reunite with his wife [[Penelope]] (Hathaway).

Nolan began writing the script in March 2024 and secured the project with [[Universal Pictures]] by October. ''The Odyssey'' was announced in December. Casting took place from late 2024 through early 2025, and Damon was confirmed for the lead role in February 2025. Nolan studied several [[Translations of the Odyssey|translations of the ''Odyssey'']]. He depicted a realistic interpretation of [[Greek mythology]], drawing inspiration from the epic historical films ''[[Andrei Rublev (film)|Andrei Rublev]]'' (1966) and ''[[Ran (film)|Ran]]'' (1985), as well as the films of special effects artist [[Ray Harryhausen]]. Filming took place from February to August 2025 in [[Morocco]], [[Greece]], [[Italy]], [[Scotland]], [[Iceland]], [[Western Sahara]], and [[Malta]], as well as at the [[Universal Studios Lot]] in Los Angeles. With an estimated budget of $250&nbsp;million, it is among the most expensive films of [[Christopher Nolan filmography|Nolan's career]], and the first feature film ever to be shot entirely on [[IMAX]]'s [[70 mm film#IMAX|70 mm film]] cameras.

''The Odyssey'' premiered at the [[Odeon Luxe Leicester Square]] in London on July 6, 2026, ahead of a theatrical release by Universal Pictures on July 17 in the US and the UK. It was made available in IMAX and multiple [[premium large format]]s. The film received widespread critical acclaim, with particular praise for Nolan's direction.<!-- DO NOT change to "positive"; Metacritic has defined the reception as "universal acclaim". --> It has grossed $328&nbsp;million and set numerous records for Nolan, including for some premium format pre-sales that were released a year in advance.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per WP:FILMPLOT, plot summaries for feature films should be 400 to 700 words. Word count as of July 22, 2026: 714 words.-->
{{Hatnote|The film is told in a partially [[non-linear narrative]], and begins with the [[intertitle]] "In a time of apparent magic..."}}
[[Homer's Ithaca|Ithaca]] has long since been without its king [[Odysseus]], who disappeared following the [[Trojan War]]. Several [[Suitors of Penelope|suitors]], including nobleman [[Antinous of Ithaca|Antinous]], aggressively court Odysseus's wife [[Penelope]] to claim the throne from Odysseus and Penelope's son [[Telemachus]]. Only Penelope, Telemachus, and half-blind servant [[Eumaeus]] still believe Odysseus will return. Telemachus travels with his teacher and bodyguard, Odysseus's friend [[Mentor (Odyssey)|Mentor]], to [[Menelaus]], King of [[Sparta]], to discover his father's whereabouts. Antinous learns this and conspires with other suitors to kill Telemachus upon his return and plunder Ithaca. Menelaus and his wife [[Helen of Troy|Helen]] express uncertainty about Odysseus's fate but offer Telemachus stories about him. Rumors abound that Greek civilization is in decline due to attacks by murderous "[[Sea Peoples|people from the sea]]" who violate [[Xenia (Greek)|Zeus's law]]—the mutual respect between guests and hosts.

After departing Ithaca, Odysseus and the Greeks spend ten years besieging [[Troy]]. Odysseus masterminds a plan to hide inside a large [[Trojan Horse|wooden horse]] to sneak into Troy, with [[Sinon]] left behind to present the horse as a gift. Sinon is killed, but the deception succeeds; the Greeks open the gates for Menelaus's older brother, [[Agamemnon]], to sack Troy and retrieve Helen. Agamemnon and Menelaus return home early, while Odysseus's army, including second-in-command [[Eurylochus]], follow a slower route. When searching an island for supplies, they are trapped in a cave by the [[cyclops]] [[Polyphemus]], who feasts upon the soldiers. Eventually, they blind Polyphemus and escape, only to hear him cry out to his father [[Poseidon]], who curses the crew with harsh sea conditions. They then land on [[Aeaea]], where giant [[Laestrygonians]] kill most of their party. The survivors rebel, leaving Odysseus with their ship as they seek refuge with the witch [[Circe]], who turns them into pigs. After Odysseus persuades her to change his men back, Circe warns that to reach Ithaca, Odysseus must cross [[Oceanus#Geography|Oceanus]] into [[Greek underworld|Hades]].

Odysseus meets the [[Shade (mythology)|shade]] of [[Tiresias]], who predicts that, by divine will, only Odysseus will return home. Odysseus also encounters Sinon's shade, who reproaches Odysseus for causing his death, and reveals Antinous's treachery; he was manipulated into taking Antinous's place in the Greek army. Lastly, Agamemnon's shade reveals that his wife [[Clytemnestra]] murdered him upon his return as revenge for sacrificing their daughter, [[Iphigenia]], to [[Artemis]] for fair seas. Chased from Hades by Odysseus's fallen army, Odysseus and various crewmen follow Tiresias's guidance to survive encounters with [[Charybdis]], [[Scylla]], and [[the Sirens]]. On [[Thrinacia]], Odysseus warns his men not to kill the [[cattle of Helios|cattle of the Sun God]], as Tiresias foretold Odysseus's men would die from doing so, but the starving men slaughter the cattle. When they set sail, Poseidon's storms sink the ship, killing all but Odysseus. He washes ashore on [[Ogygia]], where the [[nymph]] [[Calypso (mythology)|Calypso]], infatuated with him, drugs him with amnesia-inducing [[Lotus-eaters|lotus flowers]].

Seven years later, Calypso feels pity for Odysseus's longing for his family, and aids him in remembering his past. She instructs Odysseus to make a raft and give himself to the sea, facing Poseidon's punishment. He miraculously washes ashore in Ithaca, learning from Eumaeus that suitors sent mercenaries to assassinate Telemachus and Mentor. Mentor dies, but Odysseus rescues Telemachus and kills the assassins, naming himself "Sinon". After Odysseus's old dog [[Argos (dog)|Argos]] recognizes him, Argos peacefully dies; Telemachus recognizes Odysseus as his father. During a tense feast, Odysseus shows Antinous he knows Antinous betrayed Sinon; Antinous thrashes Odysseus before Penelope intervenes. After the meal, still disguised, he confesses to Penelope his guilt for violating Zeus's law, killing innocent Trojans, and desecrating Athena's statue after killing her priestess: Athena had assumed the priestess's form to punish him in visions. He further reveals that the rumored attackers from the sea were the Greeks themselves, and begs Penelope's forgiveness. Penelope announces her readiness to remarry and instructs the suitors to return at dawn. 

Penelope declares she will marry whoever can string Odysseus's bow and shoot an arrow through a row of axe heads. The suitors all fail, but Odysseus succeeds and reveals his identity. The hall erupts in battle, Odysseus slaying the challenging suitors. As the remaining suitors surrender, Odysseus kills Antinous. Odysseus cedes his throne to Telemachus and leaves Ithaca with Penelope to seek peace in exile, sailing west to honor his dead soldiers. The pair expect civilization's renewal, and muse that time will forget Odysseus's mistakes.

== Production ==
=== Development and writing ===
[[File:ChristopherNolan-byPhilipRomano (cropped).jpg|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Picture of director Christopher Nolan smiling at the premiere of ''The Odyssey'' in New York|Director, writer, and producer [[Christopher Nolan]] at the film's premiere in New York City]]
By November 2023, director [[Christopher Nolan]] had yet to decide what his next film would be after ''[[Oppenheimer (film)|Oppenheimer]]'' (2023). While he was receptive to various ideas for his follow-up, the director felt he would have to "own it completely".<ref name="NolanThomasTease" /> After winning several [[Academy Awards]] for ''Oppenheimer'' in March 2024, including [[Academy Award for Best Picture|Best Picture]] and [[Academy Award for Best Director|Best Director]],<ref name="VarietyMar2024" /><ref name="BelloniMar2024" /> Nolan and his wife and producing partner, [[Emma Thomas]], felt the success provided them the opportunity to explore what Nolan's next film would be.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /><ref name="NolanThomasTease" /> Nolan began writing the script shortly after,<ref name="VarietyMar2024" /><ref name="BelloniMar2024" /> but Thomas said in May they had yet to deliberate on the practicalities of the production.<ref name="NolanThomasTease" /> Nolan was revealed in October to be developing his next film at [[Universal Pictures]], after working with that studio on ''Oppenheimer''. Nolan was confirmed to be writing the script and producing once again with Thomas through their production company, [[Syncopy Inc.|Syncopy]], and filming was scheduled to begin in early 2025. Unlike ''Oppenheimer'', which Universal acquired at auction, ''The Odyssey'' was set up directly at the studio and given a release date of July 17, 2026.<ref name="DevelopmentRevealTHR" /><ref name="DevelopmentRevealDeadline" />

The [[logline]] and additional details of its premise were kept largely secret compared to Nolan's previous films, which prompted much speculation about its subject matter.<ref name="Pattinson" /><ref name="Theron" /> This included rumors that it would either be a vampire [[period piece]], a [[Reboot (fiction)|reboot]] of the 1983 helicopter action-thriller film ''[[Blue Thunder]]'',<ref name="NolanEarlyRumors" /><ref name="Pattinson" /><ref name="Theron" /> or an adaptation of the British spy television series ''[[The Prisoner]]'' (1967–68), which Nolan was previously attached to in 2009,<ref name="VarietyMar2024" /><ref name="DevelopmentRevealTHR" /> but all of these were debunked.<ref name="Pattinson" /><ref name="Theron" /><ref name="DevelopmentRevealTHR" /> Nolan later reflected the "disadvantage of never telling people what we're doing" was the invention and circulation of rumors, though he nonetheless enjoyed them.<ref name="NolanEarlyRumors" /> In December 2024, Universal announced Nolan's new film as ''The Odyssey'', an adaptation of [[Homer]]'s ancient Greek [[epic poem]], the ''[[Odyssey]]''. The studio described it as a "mythic action epic" that would be filmed worldwide.<ref name="TheOdysseyAnnounceDeadline" /><ref name="TheOdysseyAnnounceTHR" /> Nolan was previously attached to direct ''[[Troy (film)|Troy]]'' (2004)—based on Homer's ''Odyssey'' predecessor the ''[[Iliad]]''—that [[Wolfgang Petersen]] had developed for [[Warner Bros. Pictures]], before Petersen chose to direct ''Troy'' and Nolan subsequently made ''[[Batman Begins]]'' (2005).<ref name="NolanTroyConfirm" /><ref name="NolanTroy" /><ref name="NolanTroyGoyer" /> Nolan believed he was "in a little over [his] head" for ''Troy'', but felt he had become more qualified to adapt the "sweeping story" of the ''Odyssey'' after working on several large-scale productions during the following 20 years.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /><ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" /> ''The Odyssey'' was expected to be the most expensive film of [[Christopher Nolan filmography|Nolan's career]] with a net budget of $250&nbsp;million.<ref name="IMAX70mmPresalesTHR" /><ref name="TeaserLeakTHR" />

{{Quote box|width=20%|align=left|quote="I remember seeing a school play of [the ''Odyssey''] when I was five or six years old. The older kids were doing it. I remember [[the Sirens]] and him being strapped to the mast and things like that. I think it's in all of us, really. And when you start to break down the text and adapt it, you find that all of these other films – and all the films I've worked on – you know, they're all from the ''Odyssey''. [[Emma Thomas|Emma <nowiki>[Thomas]</nowiki>]] said it best when we first announced the project: it's foundational."|source=— Christopher Nolan on how his early interest in the ''Odyssey'' influenced his prior films<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|50}}}}

Nolan described ''The Odyssey'' as the largest and most challenging production of his career due to its scale.<ref name="Nolan60Minutes" /> He explained that he chose to adapt the ''Odyssey'' because it had not been adapted into a large-scale production from a major Hollywood studio, calling it "an odd gap in movie history".<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> Nolan and Thomas described ''The Odyssey'' as "foundational" because it incorporates aspects of the horror, [[Mystery film|mystery]], romance, and thriller genres of stories.<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|59}}<ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" /> Nolan read and studied the ''Odyssey'' and [[Translations of the Odyssey|several of its translations]], including those by classicists [[Emily Wilson (classicist)|Emily Wilson]], [[E. V. Rieu]], and [[Robert Fagles]].<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /><ref name="NolanNYTJun2026" /> He wrote the script in a [[nonlinear narrative|non-linear fashion]].<ref name="NolanColbertTrailer" /> While the script was said to be "very faithful" to Homer's text, Nolan made some alterations, such as more prominently featuring Odysseus's faithful dog [[Argos (dog)|Argos]]; more interactions between Odysseus and Telemachus; humanizing the goddess and witch [[Circe]] to be unsettling yet sympathetic; and complicating the reunion between [[Helen of Troy]] and Spartan king [[Menelaus]].<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> He was interested in portraying Odysseus's cleverness and inventiveness, particularly citing his characterization in Wilson's [[Odyssey (Emily Wilson translation)|2017 translation of the ''Odyssey'']].<ref name="CharacterFirstLooks" />

To combine the ''Odyssey''{{'s}} fantastical elements with Nolan's use of "tactile realism", the director chose to take a realistic approach in depicting the evidence and actions of the gods through natural phenomena once considered "supernatural" during the story's time period. Nolan called this a "big breakthrough creatively".<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|59}}<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> Nolan cited [[Andrei Tarkovsky]]'s ''[[Andrei Rublev (film)|Andrei Rublev]]'' (1966) and [[Akira Kurosawa]]'s ''[[Ran (film)|Ran]]'' (1985) as major inspirations, particularly regarding its visual style and atmospheric tone.<ref name="NolanNYTJun2026" /> Nolan also drew inspiration from various [[Greek mythology]]–inspired films he watched in his youth, particularly the works of animator and special effects artist [[Ray Harryhausen]].<ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" /> Nolan had considered casting actors to portray [[Greek gods]] throwing thunderbolts from [[Mount Olympus]], but found depicting the characters' fear of the gods' actions provided a more immersive experience than outright showing the gods.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" />

=== Casting ===
By October 2024, [[Matt Damon]] was in talks to star after collaborating with Nolan on ''Oppenheimer'' and ''[[Interstellar (film)|Interstellar]]'' (2014).<ref name="DevelopmentRevealTHR" /><ref name="DevelopmentRevealDeadline" /> Damon was confirmed to star later in October when [[Tom Holland]] was cast.<ref name="Holland" /> Previous Nolan collaborators [[Anne Hathaway]]—who starred in ''[[The Dark Knight Rises]]'' (2012) and ''Interstellar''—and [[Robert Pattinson]], who starred in ''[[Tenet]]'' (2020), joined the cast in November,<ref name="HathawayZendaya" /><ref name="Pattinson" /> alongside [[Zendaya]],<ref name="HathawayZendaya" /> [[Lupita Nyong'o]],<ref name="Nyong'o" /> and [[Charlize Theron]].<ref name="Theron" /> Damon, Holland, Hathaway, and Pattinson were all set for lead roles, with Nyong'o and Zendaya having supporting roles.<ref name="Pattinson" /> Pattinson was the only actor who requested to read the script before signing on.<ref name="GQCoverStory" />

Further casting took place throughout early 2025, with prior Nolan collaborators [[Benny Safdie]],<ref name="Safdie" /> [[Elliot Page]], [[Himesh Patel]], [[Bill Irwin]],<ref name="Jan2025Castings" /> [[Josh Stewart]],<ref name="Stewart" /> Anthony Molinari,<ref name="Molinari" /> and [[James Remar]] joining.<ref name="Remar" /> Patel was quietly offered his part in December 2024.<ref name="PatelJun2026" /> Additional actors cast alongside them were [[Jon Bernthal]],<ref name="Bernthal" /> [[John Leguizamo]],<ref name="Leguizamo" /> [[Samantha Morton]],<ref name="Jan2025Castings" /> [[Jesse Garcia]], [[Will Yun Lee]],<ref name="GarciaLee" /> [[Rafi Gavron]], [[Shiloh Fernandez]],<ref name="GavronFernandez" /> [[Mia Goth]],<ref name="Goth" /> [[Corey Hawkins]],<ref name="Hawkins" /> [[Nick E. Tarabay]], Jimmy Gonzales, [[Maurice Compte]],<ref name="TarabayGonzalesCompte" /> [[Michael Vlamis]], [[Iddo Goldberg]],<ref name="VlamisGoldberg" /> [[Ryan Hurst]],<ref name="Hurst" /> [[Jovan Adepo]],<ref name="IcelandFilming" /> and [[Sean Avery]].<ref name="ScotlandFilmingContinued" /> [[Cosmo Jarvis]] had also been cast,<ref name="Jarvis" /> but dropped out shortly before filming began due to a scheduling conflict and was subsequently replaced by [[Logan Marshall-Green]].<ref name="Marshall-Green" /> Marshall-Green initially self-taped in December, and was offered a callback in January which he declined; two months later, he was directly offered the role when Jarvis dropped out.<ref>[https://youtube/P4NDsUl7l2o Logan Marshall-Green: Turning Down Christopher Nolan, Terminator 3 & Joining The Odyssey]</ref> [[Skeet Ulrich]] said he unsuccessfully read for a role in early February alongside two other actors.<ref name="Ulrich" />

Universal confirmed that Damon was portraying [[Odysseus]] in the middle of February, ahead of the start of filming later that month. This came after some rumors had circulated that Holland could be playing the role;<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLook" /> Holland was later confirmed to be portraying Odysseus's son, [[Telemachus]].<ref name="TeaserTrailer" /> To prepare for his role, Damon underwent an extensive training regimen and a strict diet to become "lean but strong", which included no longer consuming gluten and reducing his weight to {{convert|167|lb|abbr=off}}.<ref name="DamonWorkout" /> He also grew a full beard for a year, after Nolan declined to use artificial facial hair because he wanted to capture "the physicality of real hair".<ref name="DamonBeard" /><ref name="DamonWorkout" />

=== Design and special effects ===
Production designer [[Ruth De Jong]] and costume designer [[Ellen Mirojnick]] returned to work with Nolan from ''Oppenheimer''.<ref name="FavignanaFilming" /><ref name="DeJong" /><ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> Mirojnick discussed her emphasis "on tactility—giving every piece of linen, leather, armour and sandal the texture and dimensions of lived history." Her costume design extended to the [[Mycenaean Greece]] costumes for both kings and soldiers, and the draping costumes of both [[Athena]] and [[Calypso (mythology)|Calypso]].<ref name="MaterialWorldDesigns" /> De Jong designed the sets and ornaments, including the {{convert|36|foot|m|abbr=off}} tall and 8,800-pound model of the [[Trojan Horse]] that was transported in 27 crates to the sea shore.<ref name="MaterialWorldDesigns" /><ref name="TrojanHorsePremiere" /> To depict the armor, weapons, and ships, Nolan said that the production thoroughly researched the "very fragmentary archeological records" of the [[Bronze Age]] and [[Ancient Greece]] during Homer's life. On the production choices, Nolan explained that the "oldest depictions of Homeric characters tend to be depicted in the manner of people living in Homer's time. So there's a pretty strong case there for portraying things that way because that's the way the first audience received the story." Nolan added that Mirojnick sought to convey through the armor worn by [[Agamemnon]] that he had an elevated status by using materials more expensive to obtain, such as [[Hepatizon|blackened bronze]], silver, and [[sulfur]].<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> The Cyclops was achieved via a combination of [[animatronics]], [[puppetry]], and an on-set performance by Bill Irwin, with its design taking influence from the [[Francisco Goya]] painting ''[[Saturn Devouring His Son]]''.<ref name="LATimesOdysseyInterview" /> Likewise the sequence where [[Circe]] transforms Odysseus' crew into pigs was achieved though a combination of animatronics, puppetry, [[Prosthetic makeup|makeup effects]] and [[stage magic]] techniques.<ref name="MortonCirceVF" /><ref name="OdysseyHairMakeupAllure" /> [[Wētā Workshop]] provided special effects for the production.<ref name="WētāWorkshop" />

=== Filming ===
[[File:IMAX Blimp 2026.jpg|thumb|upright=1|''The Odyssey'' was the first feature film shot entirely on [[70 mm film#IMAX|IMAX 70 mm film]] cameras; a soundproof blimp (pictured) was specially constructed to mute the camera's sound during dialogue scenes.<ref name="NolanNYTJun2026" />]]
[[Principal photography]] lasted for 91 days between February 25 and August 5, 2025,<ref name="GQCoverStory" /><ref name="MoroccoFilmingStart" /> using the [[working title]] ''Charlie's Tale'',<ref name="IcelandProductionReimbursed" /> and [[Wrap (filmmaking)|wrapped]] nine days ahead of schedule.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> [[Location shooting]] primarily took place in six countries: Morocco, Greece, Italy, Iceland, Scotland, and the United States.<ref name="GQCoverStory" /><ref name="TrailerST" /> Nolan sought to embrace the "physicality of the real world" by filming on location to help inform the storytelling and capture "how hard those journeys would have been for people. And the leap of faith that was being made in an unmapped, uncharted world." The director felt that the shifting weather conditions amplified this practical approach due to the challenges they posed.<ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" /> [[Hoyte van Hoytema]] reunited with Nolan as cinematographer.<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLook" />

After using [[70 mm film#IMAX|IMAX 70 mm film]] extensively since ''[[The Dark Knight]]'' (2008), ''The Odyssey'' became the first feature film shot entirely with [[IMAX film cameras]], primarily using a newly manufactured lighter and quieter version Nolan had specifically developed for the production alongside older versions.<ref name="TheOdysseyAnnounceTHR" /><ref name="IMAXFilmCamerasTHR" /><ref name="IMAXFilmCamerasFC" /> For close-up scenes requiring audible dialogue, the IMAX cameras were often concealed in large [[sound blimp]]s on set, while a system of mirrors was set up to help the actors maintain eye contact when the cameras obscured their view.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /><ref name="IMAXFilmCamerasFC" /> Over {{convert|2|e6ft|km|abbr=off}} of IMAX 70&nbsp;mm film was used to shoot ''The Odyssey''.<ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" /> As a single IMAX camera can only hold roughly three minutes of film, actors had to pause during longer scenes while the camera was reloaded before resuming their performances.<ref name="VarietyIMAX" /><ref name="GuardianIMAX" />

The first scenes were filmed in late February at the [[Aït Benhaddou]] village near [[Ouarzazate]], Morocco,<ref name="MoroccoFilmingStart" /><ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|56–57}} to depict the city of [[Troy]] at the end of the [[Trojan War]] in an appetizer sequence before the bulk of ''The Odyssey'',<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|56–57}}<ref name="DakhlaFilming" /> as well as in the cities of [[Essaouira]] and [[Marrakesh]].<ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseBloomberg" /><ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseTheTimes" /> Van Hoytema rigged numerous portable LEDs to emulate the specific firelight color temperature for the midnight raid of Troy, allowing Nolan to film in any direction.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> Filming took place in the [[Messenia]] unit of the [[Peloponnese (region)|Peloponnese]] region in Greece, from March 10 to 21, at [[Pylos]], the [[Methoni Castle]], the Almyrolakkos beach in [[Gialova|Yialova]], and Nestor's Cave in the [[Voidokilia beach]] for scenes featuring the [[Cyclops]] [[Polyphemus]], as well as at an archaeological palace site in [[Acrocorinth]], [[Corinth (modern city)|Corinth]].<ref name="PylosFilming" /><ref name="PylosFilming2" /><ref name="GreeceFilming" /> The production partnered with [[Faliro House Productions]]'s NAF subsidiary for filming in Greece,<ref name="GreeceFilming" /> which lasted three weeks.<ref name="GreeceProductionRebate" /> The production received over {{Currency|6.5|code=euro}} million ($7.5&nbsp;million) in funding from Greece's Hellenic Film and Audiovisual Center S.A. (Creative Greece), as part of its approximate {{Currency|140|code=euro}} million (${{To USD|140|Greece|year=2025}} million) investment in over 200 local and international productions,<ref name="GreeceProductionInvestment" /><ref name="CastingBacklashGR" /> as well as a 40% cash [[Rebate (marketing)|rebate]].<ref name="GreeceProductionRebate" /><ref name="GreeceProductionRebate2" />

By March 27, the production moved to the [[Aegadian Islands]] in Sicily, Italy, to film on the island of [[Favignana]], believed to be the location known as the "goat island" in the poem.<ref name="FilmingLocations" /><ref name="FavignanaFilming" /> Water-borne filming subsequently took place from April 15 to May 15 throughout the [[Aeolian Islands]] of [[Lipari]], [[Basiluzzo]], and [[Vulcano]] for scenes featuring the mythological island of [[Aeolia (mythical island)|Aeolia]]. Filming in these regions was subject to certain safeguarding restrictions from [[local ordinance]]s,<ref name="AeolianIslandsFilming" /><ref name="FilmingLocations" /> with [[Fremantle (company)|Fremantle]]'s Italian production company Wildside servicing the shoot.<ref name="FilmingLocations" /> For the maritime sequences, the production used the largest modern [[Viking longship]], the ''[[Draken Harald Hårfagre]]'', which doubled as an ancient Greek warship that the actors portraying Odysseus's crew commanded,<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|55}}<ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" /><ref name="DrakenScotlandFilming" /> and was sailed to each of the filming locations.<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> Nolan described filming at sea as a particularly primal experience.<ref name="Empire2MillionFilm" />

Halfway through shooting by early May, filming took place at the Falls Lake [[water tank]] on the [[Universal Studios Lot]] in Los Angeles, the production's only set constructed on a studio [[sound stage]],<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|55}}<ref name="LosAngelesFilming" /><ref name="GQCoverStory" /> before shooting at [[Findlater Castle]] in [[Moray]], [[Scotland]], in early June.<ref name="ScotlandFilming" /> For ten days in late June, the production partnered with [[Truenorth (production company)|Truenorth]] in remote regions of [[Iceland]], including the harbour [[Landeyjahöfn]], the [[Hjörleifshöfði]] mountain, the river [[Markarfljót]], and the [[Snæfellsnes]] peninsula,<ref name="IcelandFilming" /><ref name="IcelandProductionReimbursed" /> primarily to shoot scenes on their [[black sand]] beaches for the [[Greek underworld]] of Hades,<ref name="EmpireCoverStoryJan2026" />{{rp|55}}<ref name="TrailerST" /> before returning to Scotland for scenes at the [[Buckie]] Harbour along the [[Moray Firth]] coast and at the secluded Sunnyside Beach near [[Cullen, Moray]].<ref name="ScotlandFilmingContinued" /><ref name="DrakenScotlandFilming" /> Filming took place in the [[Culbin Forest]] from July 3 to 16,<ref name="ScotlandFilmingContinued" /> where the giant [[Laestrygonians]] were portrayed using a variety of practical techniques including [[forced perspective]], [[wire-flying|wire work]],<ref name="EmpireLaestrygonianFight" /> and having the seven-foot tall [[stuntmen]] playing the giants stand opposite the casts below five feet tall [[body doubles]].<ref name="DamonStuntDouble" /> The Laestrygonians are portrayed as having more technologically sophisticated armour and weaponry than anything previously encountered by Odysseus to emphasise the brutality of the encounter and test his leadership.<ref name="EmpireLaestrygonianFight" /> Filming returned to Findlater Castle through July 25,<ref name="ScotlandFilmingContinued" /> with Theron filming her scenes during the last two weeks in July.<ref name="TheronJun2025" /> Some filming also took place in [[Malta]] for [[Calypso's Cave]],<ref name="MaltaFilming" /><ref name="FilmingWrap" /> before concluding back at the Universal lot for most of the last week of filming.<ref name="GQCoverStory" /><ref name="FilmingWrap" /> Some filming was also expected to occur in Ireland,<ref name="IrelandFilming" /> as well as in the United Kingdom.<ref name="FilmingLocations" />

Filming took place between July 17 and 22, with Damon and Zendaya, at the White Dune near [[Dakhla, Western Sahara]],<ref name="DakhlaFilming" /><ref name="DakhlaFilmingStart" /> a territory under [[Moroccan-occupied Western Sahara|Moroccan occupation]] since 1975.<ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseGuardian" /><ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseIndependent" /><ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseForbes" /> The UN-recognized [[Polisario Front]] and organizers of the [[Sahara International Film Festival]] decried the decision to film in Western Sahara as [[wikt:whitewash#Verb|whitewashing]] Morocco's colonialism.<ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseBloomberg" /><ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseGuardian" /> The festival's organizers called for the production to be halted, but filming in the territory had already concluded.<ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseGuardian" /><ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseIndependent" /> They subsequently released a statement calling on Nolan to obtain consent from "the legal representatives of the [[Sahrawi people]]" or otherwise remove the scenes shot in Dakhla. This statement was signed by several prominent figures, including Spanish actors [[Carlos Bardem|Carlos]] and [[Javier Bardem]], [[Luis Tosar]], [[Carolina Yuste]], and [[Juan Diego Botto]], Spanish filmmakers [[Rodrigo Sorogoyen]] and [[Icíar Bollaín]], and Sahrawi human rights defender [[Elghalia Djimi]].<ref name="DakhlaFilmingResponseElDiario" /><ref name="DakhlaFilmingResponseRollingStone" /><ref name="DakhlaFilmingResponseFiSahara" /> Meanwhile, the [[Moroccan Cinematographic Center]] called ''The Odyssey'' an important production to promote the [[Cinema of Morocco|Moroccan film industry]] and said that it was the first major American film to be filmed in that territory.<ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseBloomberg" /><ref name="DahklaFilmingResponseTheTimes" />

=== Visual effects ===
[[DNEG]] provided the [[visual effects]] for ''The Odyssey'',<ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> with visual effects supervisor [[Andrew Jackson (visual effects)|Andrew Jackson]] and editor [[Jennifer Lame]] returning from previous Nolan productions.<ref name="Lame" /><ref name="ArtOfVFX" /> Unlike the Cyclops and Laestrygonians, the six headed [[Scylla]] was a predominantly digital effect, with Nolan stating the creature was inspired by the works of [[Guillermo del Toro]], saying, "What I learned from him is that a monster is not a monster. You have to approach them the way you approach any other character."<ref name="LATimesOdysseyInterview" /> A ratchet system was devised to lift stunt performers into the air for the Scylla's attack, while the nearby [[whirlpool]] was created by having [[jet ski]]s ride in circles as reference material to be replaced later by digital effects.<ref name="YahooOdysseyBTS" />

== Music ==
{{Main|The Odyssey (soundtrack){{!}}''The Odyssey'' (soundtrack)}}
By April 2025, [[Ludwig Göransson]] was composing the musical score for ''The Odyssey'' after previously working with Nolan on ''Tenet'' and ''Oppenheimer''.<ref name="Göransson" /> Nolan wanted to avoid the typical orchestra music commonly heard in other [[sword-and-sandal]] films. To "evoke the materiality" and setting of the [[Bronze Age]], Göransson included in the score: various bronze [[gong]]s layered with synthesizers; bronze instruments; at Nolan's suggestion, the sound of the ancient [[lyre]] for when Odysseus plucks his bow,<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> and the ancient [[aulos]]; [[Albanian iso-polyphony|Albanian iso-polyphony folk chorals]] and flutes recorded by Albanian shepherds; and cacophonies of sheet metal alarm drums.<ref name="GöranssonInstruumentsRS" /><ref name="BlakeScore" /><ref name="NolanVultureOverwhelm" /><ref name="Webb">{{Cite web |last=Webb |first=Marcus |date=July 13, 2026 |title=Inside Ludwig Göransson's ''The Odyssey'' Score: Ancient Instruments, James Blake and No Traditional Orchestra |url=https://www.musictimes.com/articles/112292/20260713/inside-ludwig-goranssons-odyssey-score-ancient-instruments-james-blake-no-traditional.htm |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20260714011339/https://www.musictimes.com/articles/112292/20260713/inside-ludwig-goranssons-odyssey-score-ancient-instruments-james-blake-no-traditional.htm |archive-date=July 14, 2026 |access-date=July 20, 2026 |website=Music Times}}</ref>

Musicians Callum Armstrong and Rosa Fragorapti played replicas of an ancient  [[aulos]] and [[lyre]]. Due to the obsolescence of the instruments, they had to research ancient source material to use them properly.<ref name="GöranssonInstruumentsRS" /> English electronic musician [[James Blake (musician)|James Blake]] and American rapper [[Travis Scott]] (who appears in the film) also contributed vocals to the score, and the two collaborated with Göransson on an original song, "When I'm Home", which plays over the closing credits.<ref name="SoundtrackDetails" /><ref name="BlakeScore" /><ref name="THRCastGuide" /> Nolan is credited as a co-writer of the song.<ref name="THRCastGuide" />

The soundtrack album was released digitally by Universal's [[Back Lot Music]] label on July 17, 2026, coinciding with the release date. Record manufacturer Mutant is set to release a [[double album]] CD by August 14, 2026, and a triple [[Vinyl record|vinyl]] version set for release on July 31, 2026.<ref name="SoundtrackRelease" />

== Marketing ==
[[File:Matt Damon as Odysseus for The Odyssey (2026 film).jpeg|thumb|upright=0.75|alt=Picture of actor Matt Damon wearing a red-plumed Corinthian helmet, robed armor, and a gauntlet as Odysseus, standing in front of a wall with Greek murals.|Promotional image which revealed star [[Matt Damon]] in costume as [[Odysseus]] for the first time. Its design was noted for historical inaccuracies.<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLookResponses" />]]
Before the start of filming in February 2025, Universal Pictures released a "surprise" first-look still image of Damon in costume as Odysseus.<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLook" /> Jen Juneau at ''[[People (magazine)|People]]'' described Damon's side-profile pose as "brooding" and "intimidating".<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLookPeople" /> The costume—particularly its [[Corinthian helmet]] with a red [[Plume (feather)|plume]] in place of a [[boar's tusk helmet]]—was labeled by various commentators and historians as historically inaccurate, both in terms of the armor Odysseus would have worn around the time the ''Odyssey'' takes place and of what is described in the ''Iliad''.<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLookResponses" /> ''[[/Film]]''{{'s}} Jeremy Mathai felt the image did not provide much insight into the tone or Nolan's approach to the source material, and acknowledged the costume's similarities to ancient Greek attire typically depicted in films.<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLook/Film" /> Joe George at ''[[Den of Geek]]'' felt Nolan paid homage to the [[New Hollywood]] films from his childhood, such as ''[[The 300 Spartans]]'' (1962) and ''[[Jason and the Argonauts (film)|Jason and the Argonauts]]'' (1963), especially animator [[Ray Harryhausen]]'s stop-motion visual effects work on the latter. George said while Nolan's "decision to veer towards [[old Hollywood]] instead of historical accuracy might surprise some", the director's previous films incorporated aspects of his influences while still making each "feel original and of the moment".<ref name="DamonOdysseusFirstLookDoG" />

In July 2025, one year before ''The Odyssey''{{'s}} release, a 70-second teaser trailer featuring Holland and Bernthal debuted ahead of screenings for Universal's ''[[Jurassic World Rebirth]]''.<ref name="TeaserTrailer" /><ref name="TeaserLeakVariety" /> The teaser leaked online soon after,<ref name="TeaserLeakTHR" /><ref name="TeaserLeakVariety" /> which Greg Evans at ''[[The Independent]]'' said sent some fans "into a frenzy" due to its high anticipation.<ref name="TeaserTrailerIndependent" /> Ryan Britt of ''[[Men's Journal]]'' believed this was warranted and described the teaser's "haunting nature" as promising "a myth actually brought to life". He highlighted Bernthal's "ominous" narration with a "touch of naturalism and realism", noting that Odysseus is talked about more than he is depicted.<ref name="TeaserTrailerMJ" /> ''[[Vanity Fair (magazine)|Vanity Fair]]''{{'s}} Eléa Guilleminault-Bauer felt the teaser previewed ''The Odyssey'' "through suggestion and cleverly elliptical editing".<ref name="TeaserTrailerVF" /> ''/Film''{{'s}} Rick Stevenson wrote that the teaser continued Nolan's tradition of using an "often cryptic, vibes-first trailer, meant more to tease the mood of the film than to give away much in the way of plot or detail", but also found it to be "fairly substantive" compared to the similar teasers for some of Nolan's previous films.<ref name="TeaserTrailer/Film" /> Esther Zuckerman at ''[[The New York Times]]'' felt the teaser was "coy about revealing too much" about the adaptation.<ref name="TeaserTrailerNYT" />

An extended six-minute prologue, showcasing Odysseus and his men using the [[Trojan Horse]] at the end of the [[Trojan War]], debuted on December 12, 2025, in front of IMAX 70{{nbsp}}mm screenings of [[Warner Bros. Pictures]]'s films ''[[One Battle After Another]]'' and ''[[Sinners (2025 film)|Sinners]]'', followed by ''[[Avatar: Fire and Ash]]'' upon its release on December 19.<ref name="Dec2025PrologueTrailer" /> Tim Robey at ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'' felt the prologue was a surprise for some audiences that promised a "gritty, gruelling, and aptly awe-inspiring" adaptation, and compared the "stifling claustrophobia" of being inside the Trojan Horse to sequences of capsized ships in Nolan's film ''[[Dunkirk (2017 film)|Dunkirk]]'' (2017). He said the "immediacy of this sequence as a race against time is very Nolan", yet felt it did not supersede ''The Dark Knight''{{'s}} bank heist prologue.<ref name="PrologueCommentaryTelegraph" /> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]''{{'s}} Daniel D'Addario compared the prologue to the Trojan Horse itself, describing it as a "carefully wrought" [[story within a story]]. He commended the footage for "delicately intercutting" from the chaotic warfare to the "precision of the gears gradually turning in the Trojan wall's door".<ref name="PrologueCommentaryVariety" /> A two-minute teaser trailer debuted ahead of standard ''Fire and Ash'' showings and was released online on December 22.<ref name="TrailerUSAToday" /><ref name="Dec2025PrologueTrailer" /> Commentators noted its scope compared to earlier marketing.<ref name="TrailerIW" /><ref name="TrailerTF" /><ref name="TrailerEmpire" /><ref name="TrailerHT" /> Edward Segarra at ''[[USA Today]]'' called the trailer montage "suspenseful",<ref name="TrailerUSAToday" /> ''[[IndieWire]]''{{'s}} Kate Erbland highlighted the "inherent drama and pain" of Odysseus's journey,<ref name="TrailerIW" /> while ''[[Empire (magazine)|Empire]]''{{'s}} Ben Travis was impressed by the "wet and wild photography" but questioned the continued absence of supporting cast members Pattinson, Zendaya, Nyong'o, and Theron.<ref name="TrailerEmpire" /> The trailer accumulated 121.4&nbsp;million global views within 24 hours across [[TikTok]] (27%), YouTube (26%), Facebook (21%), [[Instagram]] (18%), and [[X (formerly Twitter)]] (10%), making it the eighth most-viewed trailer of 2025. This surpassed the trailer views for Universal's ''[[Wicked: For Good]]'' (113&nbsp;million) and more than doubled the views for the first ''Oppenheimer'' trailer during the same time period.<ref name="TrailerViews" />

== Reception ==
=== Box office ===
[[File:The Odyssey (2026 film) IMAX 70 mm film reels.jpg|thumb|upright=1|IMAX 70 mm film reels of ''The Odyssey'' at a [[Celebration Cinema]] in [[Grand Rapids, Michigan]]]]
{{As of|2026|7|22|df=US}}, ''The Odyssey'' has grossed $164&nbsp;million in the United States and Canada, and $164&nbsp;million in other territories, for a worldwide total of $328&nbsp;million.<ref name="BOM" /><ref name="NUM" /> Produced on a $250&nbsp;million budget, ''The Odyssey'' is among the most expensive of [[Christopher Nolan filmography|Nolan's career]] and is [[List of most expensive films|one of the most expensive films ever made]].<ref name="IMAX70mmPresalesTHR" /> 

==== Pre-sale ticket records and projections ====
Tickets for select opening weekend IMAX 70&nbsp;mm screenings were made available on July 17, 2025, one year before its release, which was considered an unprecedented move by a major film distributor.<ref name="IMAX70mmPresalesTHR" /> Several of these showings sold out within the first 12 hours of their availability, including half of the 22 theaters available in the United States, earning approximately $1.5&nbsp;million.<ref name="IMAX70mmPresalesTHR" /><ref name="IMAX70mmPresalesDeadline" /> ''The Odyssey'' was ranked as the most anticipated film of 2026 by [[IMDb]].<ref name="IMDbMostAnticipated" /> ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' predicted that ''The Odyssey'' would become the highest-grossing film of the year, in part based on the early ticket pre-sales,<ref name="VarietyBOPrediction" /> while ''[[TheWrap]]'' predicted that it could surpass ''The Dark Knight'' and ''The Dark Knight Rises'' to become Nolan's highest-grossing film.<ref name="TheWrapBOPrediction" /> Brent Lang at ''Variety'' stated that exhibitors expected ''The Odyssey'' to be "one of the biggest hits of the summer".<ref name="VarietyPresales" /><ref name="VarietyRRating" /> He predicted that it would need to attract a wide audience due to its restrictive [[R-rating (Motion Picture Association)|R-rating]] and approximate $250&nbsp;million budget, which would make it one of the most-expensive R-rated films, ahead of ''[[Joker: Folie à Deux]]'' and ''[[Deadpool & Wolverine]]'' (both 2024).<ref name="VarietyRRating" />

Tickets for the remainder of IMAX, IMAX 70 mm, and premium format screenings went on sale on June 4, 2026, ahead of general admission tickets being made available later that month.<ref name="DeadlinePresales" /> Ticketing websites [[Fandango Media|Fandango]] and [[AMC Theatres]] experienced delayed queues, with initial wait times lasting up to an hour due to the high influx of users attempting to purchase tickets, comparable to that of the 2023 concert film ''[[Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour]]''.<ref name="VarietyPresales" /><ref name="IndieWirePresales" /><ref name="DeadlinePresales" /><ref name="TheWrapPresales" /> Emily St. Martin at the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' described attempts to purchase tickets as "an epic journey of its own".<ref name="LATPresales" /> $3.4&nbsp;million was accumulated from approximately 150,000 pre-sale tickets in the first 24 hours, making it the highest pre-sale gross in four years at that time.<ref name="BFIPresales" /> The first-day PLF pre-sales were also the highest pre-sales for a major studio release for AMC since 2022, surpassing the first-day pre-sales for any film from 2025 and early 2026.<ref name="DeadlinePresales" /> ''The Odyssey'' sold 28,000 presale tickets at the [[BFI IMAX]] in 24 hours, earning £750,000 ($1&nbsp;million). This more than doubled the £366,000 ($490,000) gross of the venue's previous record holder, ''[[Dune: Part Two]]'' (2024), and surpassed the £254,000 ($340,000) gross of ''Oppenheimer'' during the same timeframe.<ref name="BFIPresales" />

By the end of June, ''The Odyssey'' was projected to have an opening range of $80–100&nbsp;million, which Anthony D'Alessandro at ''[[Deadline Hollywood]]'' attributed to the premium format pre-sales that he said "skew[ed] heavily to Nolan's [[cinephile]] base" as well as the film having exclusive access to IMAX screens for the first three weeks of its release. He explained that the wide forecast range was because "nobody knows how high these Nolan movies will be in their opening weekend". He reported tracking data placed ''The Odyssey'' ahead of ''Oppenheimer'' as the "first choice" for [[Four-quadrant movie|male audiences over the age of 25]], while overall data tied it with the film ''[[Project Hail Mary (film)|Project Hail Mary]]'', which opened to $80.5&nbsp;million earlier in 2026.<ref name="OpeningBOProjectionDeadline" /> By the week of its release, it was projected to have an international rollout of $110&nbsp;million across 22,700 screens in 73 territories, for an estimated worldwide opening of over $200&nbsp;million.<ref name="OpeningBOProjectionDeadline2" /> IMAX CEO [[Richard Gelfond]] confirmed that by July 16, many IMAX venues had already sold out into the films fifth weekend.<ref name="VarietyIMAX70mmGelfond" />

==== Domestic and international performance ====
In the United States and Canada, ''The Odyssey'' earned $51.2&nbsp;million from its opening day, including $17.6&nbsp;million from Thursday night previews, which was the highest for any film during that time frame for the year, surpassing ''[[Toy Story 5]]'' ($17.5&nbsp;million). It went on to gross $41.66&nbsp;million and $31.7&nbsp;million during its opening weekend, for a domestic total of $124.5&nbsp;million from 3,919 theaters. This was the best domestic opening within a year; the third-best for a Nolan film, behind ''[[The Dark Knight Rises]]'' ($190.9&nbsp;million in 2012) and ''[[The Dark Knight]]'' ($158.4&nbsp;million in 2008); as well as the highest debut for Damon, surpassing ''[[The Bourne Ultimatum (film)|The Bourne Ultimatum]]'' ($69.2&nbsp;million in 2007); an R-rated Universal film, surpassing ''[[Fifty Shades of Grey (film)|Fifty Shades of Grey]]'' ($85.1&nbsp;million in 2015) and ''Oppenheimer'' ($180&nbsp;million); and the fourth highest opening for an R-rated film overall after ''[[Deadpool & Wolverine]]'' ($211.4&nbsp;million in 2024), ''[[Deadpool (film)|Deadpool]]'' ($132.4&nbsp;million in 2016), and ''[[Deadpool 2]]'' ($125.5&nbsp;million in 2018). Internationally, it debuted to $130.6&nbsp;million from 12,700 theaters in 73 markets, marking the largest foreign debut for Nolan, with the highest markets including France, Italy, India, Spain, Brazil, the Netherlands, and Poland, as well as the biggest opening for Universal in 11 markets. This resulted in a worldwide opening total of $264.1&nbsp;million, the highest ever for Nolan, surpassing ''The Dark Knight Rises'' ($249&nbsp;million).<ref name="OpeningBODeadline" /> In France, it opened on July 15, 2026, in 730 theaters and drew 230,000 viewers on its opening day, surpassing the opening of ''Oppenheimer'' (160,000 viewers).<ref name="FranceBOOpening" />

=== Critical response ===
[[File:DamonNolanThomasHathaway-byPhilipRomano.jpg|thumb|upright=1|alt=Picture of Matt Damon, Christopher Nolan, Emma Thomas, and Anne Hathaway at the premiere of ''The Odyssey'' in New York|[[Matt Damon]], [[Christopher Nolan]], [[Emma Thomas]], and [[Anne Hathaway]] at the film's premiere in New York City]]
The film received widespread critical acclaim, with praise for the performances, sets, scale, and Nolan's direction.<ref name="ReviewRoundup" /> On [[review aggregation]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]], 94% of 429 critics' reviews are positive, with an average rating of 8.7/10. The website's consensus reads: "Reinvigorating an ancient adventure with majestic sweep and sterling work by its colossal ensemble, Christopher Nolan's ''The Odyssey'' imbues myth with primal human feeling."<ref>{{Cite Rotten Tomatoes |id=the_odyssey_2026 |type=movie |access-date=July 22, 2026}}</ref> It is Nolan's highest-rated film on the website.<ref name="OpeningBODeadline" /> [[Metacritic]], which uses a weighted average, assigned it a score of 89 out of 100, based on 60 critics, indicating "universal acclaim".<ref>{{Cite Metacritic |id=the-odyssey-2026 |type=movie |access-date=July 17, 2026}}</ref> Audiences surveyed by [[CinemaScore]] gave it an average of "A" on an A-to-F scale, while 81% of those polled at [[PostTrak]] said they would definitely recommend it.<ref name="OpeningBODeadline" />

[[Peter Bradshaw]] of ''[[The Guardian]]'' gave ''The Odyssey'' five out of five, calling it "a film with thrilling ambition, boldness, seriousness, generosity and flair".<ref name="TheGuardianReview" /> [[Amy Nicholson]] of the ''[[Los Angeles Times]]'' called it "a mighty Trojan horse of [Nolan]'s thematic obsessions", writing that Nolan "refuses to tremble before the canon ... He cuts and rejiggers Homer and a bit of Virgil to transform the classical texts into his type of tale: one fixated on memory, self-identity, destructive genius and the slippage of time."<ref name="LosAngelesTimesReview" /> Guy Lodge of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' called it a "vast, thrilling, slightly aloof epic", and concluded, "There's so much to feel here at a sensory level that the film gets away with its slightly aloof, soul-skirting chill; we leave it feeling that we've been to hell and back, and exhilaratingly so."<ref name="VarietyReview" /> David Rooney of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' wrote that while ''The Odyssey'' was "uneven, and no match for the sure-footedness and intellectual complexity of ''Oppenheimer''", it is "elevated by the blindingly charismatic ensemble."<ref name="THRReview" />

Randy Myers of ''[[The Mercury News]]'' gave ''The Odyssey'' four out of four, calling it "another feather in Nolan's masterpiece cap, a grandiose achievement that fills audiences with the same child-like glee experienced when watching ''[[Jason and the Argonauts (film)|Jason and the Argonauts]]'' and ''[[Clash of the Titans (1981 film)|Clash of the Titans]]'' for the first time".<ref name="MercuryNewsReview" /> Jake Wilson of ''[[The Sydney Morning Herald]]'' gave ''The Odyssey'' four out of five, writing that Nolan "has a way with a time paradox" and that "he can take one of the oldest stories in Western culture and make it feel new".<ref name="SydneyMorningHeraldReview" /> In ''[[The Daily Telegraph]]'', [[Robbie Collin]] gave it five out of five and wrote: "Nolan and his collaborators have constructed a strange, fearsome and trailblazing machine of a movie – by some distance, the best of the year so far."<ref name="TelegraphReview" /> Alistair Harkness of ''[[The Scotsman]]'' and Clarisse Loughrey of ''[[The Independent]]'' also gave it five out of five; Harkness called it "a thrilling, thoughtful, bloody epic", writing that it "feels like Nolan's angriest film" and one of his best.<ref name="ScotsmanReview" /> Loughrey called it "a blockbuster of literally unprecedented scale", and Nolan's best film.<ref name="IndependentReview" /> In ''[[The New York Times]]'', [[Manohla Dargis]] wrote: "It is an anomalous big-studio entertainment that Nolan has filled with stars and polished to a high gleam, turning a 3,000-year-old poem into a smart, thoughtful film with Old Hollywood allure."<ref name="NYTimesReview" /> [[Justin Chang]] of ''[[The New Yorker]]'' praised Nolan's realistic approach and wrote that his rationalism provides "just the note of real-world groundedness that a great fantasy needs to achieve liftoff."<ref name="NewYorkerReview" />

However, there was some criticism for the [[sound mixing]], dialogue, and deviations from its source material.<ref name="ReviewRoundup" /> [[Richard Brody]] of ''The New Yorker'' felt that Nolan "turns an epic poem prosaic".<ref name="NewYorkerReview2" /> In ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'', [[Stephanie Zacharek]] called it an "eye-glazing dud".<ref name="TimeReview" /> Odie Henderson of ''[[The Boston Globe]]'' gave ''The Odyssey'' two and a half out of five, writing: "Pattinson has fun channeling [[Roddy McDowall]]'s snarky performance in ''[[Cleopatra (1963 film)|Cleopatra]]''. But like that epic flop, ''The Odyssey'' takes itself too seriously. The film ultimately sinks under its bleakness like a lead balloon."<ref name="BostonGlobeReview" /> Mohamed Sleiman Labat in ''[[The Guardian]]'' criticized "the decision to shoot in [[Moroccan-occupied Western Sahara]], where the Indigenous [<nowiki/>[[Sahrawis]]] people can't tell their stories without fear of imprisonment", and accused Nolan of participating in a "state-sponsored PR campaign designed to legitimise an illegal occupation".<ref name="WesternSaharaResponseGuardian" />

Some critics argued that the adaptation reshaped the poem's ancient moral framework to align with modern attitudes. In ''The New Yorker'', Richard Brody wrote that its guilt-ridden Odysseus gave the story a "readily digestible modernity" and sought to "redeem Odysseus for modern times"; he contrasted this portrayal with the Cicones episode in Book 9, in which Odysseus matter-of-factly recounts sacking a town, killing its men, and dividing the captured women and wealth among his crews.<ref name="NewYorkerReview2" /> Classicist Emily Hauser similarly argued in ''The Guardian'' that Homer's Odysseus is "not a straightforwardly moral modern leader", noting that the film omits the act of hubris that brings Poseidon's curse, arms the suitors whom Odysseus kills, alters the execution of the enslaved women, and adds a remorseful final journey absent from the poem.<ref name="GuardianHauser" /> ''[[The Economist]]'' takes a similar view, saying the film took "startling liberties" with Odysseus's character. They noted, however, that the Odyssey has been remade time and again, adapted for one era after another. They conclude that it is a typical remake of a classic work and that this movie, bar all else, is a testament to the lasting impact of the original work.<ref name="TheEconomistReview" />

=== Historical accuracy backlash ===
Leading up to its release, ''The Odyssey'' was subject to some criticism regarding the [[historicity]] of Nolan's casting choices, the use of American accents and modern English dialogue, and the production design.<ref name="NolanBacklashResponse" /> Criticism stemmed from some Greek publications, historians, and online commenters over the armor and ship designs,<ref name="TrailerST" /><ref name="InaccuraciesGR" /><ref name="InaccuraciesTheTimes" /> as well as for the casting of non-Greek actors.<ref name="CastingBacklashGR" /><ref name="CastingBacklashScreen" /><ref name="CastingBacklashGuardian" /> Some commentators labeled these changes "unrealistic" for the period depicted and a misrepresentation of ancient Greek history could "break the immersion" and "detract from important aspects of the story".<ref name="InaccuraciesTheTimes" /><ref name="InaccuraciesNYT" /> Nolan acknowledged concerns about the historical accuracy, comparing his approach to that of ''Interstellar'' as an exploration of "what is the best speculation and how can I use that to create a world?"<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /> He stated that "these conversations that happen before people see the film — they're always irrelevant, because no one having them knows what the film actually is yet."<ref name="NolanBacklashResponse" /> ''[[/Film]]''{{'s}} Jeremy Mathai declared that "practically every major criticism of ''The Odyssey'' is selective and hypocritical to the extreme".<ref name="Controversy/Film" /> John Semley at ''[[Wired (magazine)|Wired]]'' characterized the various controversies as part of a broader "[[culture war]]" response to diverse casting, similar to earlier reactions to British-American classicist [[Emily Wilson (classicist)|Emily Wilson]]'s [[Odyssey (Emily Wilson translation)|translation of the ''Odyssey'']].<ref name="ControversyWilsonWired" />

The armor design, specifically for Agamemnon, was frequently compared to the contemporary [[Batsuit]] worn by [[Bruce Wayne (Dark Knight trilogy)|Batman]] in Nolan's [[The Dark Knight trilogy|''Dark Knight'' trilogy]] (2005–2012).<ref name="TimeCoverStory" /><ref name="InaccuraciesGR" /><ref name="InaccuraciesNYT" /><ref name="NolanBacklashResponse" /> Caleb Howells at the ''[[Greek Reporter]]'' acknowledged while the armor designs were reminiscent of the [[Archaic Greece]] designs described in the ''Odyssey'', they were still inconsistent with some specific details.<ref name="InaccuraciesGR" /> [[Military historian]] Bret Devereaux surmised [[iron armour]] did not exist in Greece during the late [[Bronze Age]] in 1184 BCE,<ref name="InaccuraciesTheTimes" /> the commonly believed setting of the ''Odyssey''.<ref name="InaccuraciesGR" /> He explained that the [[List of Homeric characters|Homeric characters]] were "depicted in [[anachronistic]] armour since [[Classical antiquity|antiquity]]" and were subsequently portrayed as contemporary warriors by the late Archaic or [[Classical Greece]] periods. He said the armor was "vaguely Greek-looking" and "evocative of classical armor", but was not directly adapted from a specific armor design.<ref name="InaccuraciesTheTimes" /> The structural design and darker color palette of the [[galley]]s were noted for more closely resembling [[Viking longship]]s rather than the [[Ancient Greek ships]] used during the Trojan War, such as the [[bireme]]s or the [[penteconter]]s.<ref name="InaccuraciesGR" /><ref name="InaccuraciesTheTimes" /><ref name="InaccuraciesNYT" /> Conversely, [[popular historian]] [[Tom Holland (author)|Tom Holland]] (unrelated to the actor) stated that there was no historically accurate interpretation of Odysseus because he is a mythological figure, and opined that an "impressionist" interpretation of ancient history was an improvement over a "painstaking attempt to get every historical detail right, because it's impossible". He compared the designs favorably to the "more authentic" [[Spartan army#Clothing, arms, and armor|Spartan armor]] in the film ''[[300 (film)|300]]'' (2006), rather than those featured in ''Troy''.<ref name="InaccuraciesTheTimes" />

The casting of Kenyan actress [[Lupita Nyong'o]] as [[Helen of Troy]]—<!--DO NOT REMOVE AS THIS IS A WP:BALANCED STATEMENT THAT PROVIDES RELEVANT CONTEXT!-->often described as the most beautiful woman in the world<!--DO NOT REMOVE AS THIS IS A WP:BALANCED STATEMENT THAT PROVIDES RELEVANT CONTEXT!-->—received criticism from [[Conservatism in the United States|conservative]] commentators, such as [[Matt Walsh (political commentator)|Matt Walsh]] and [[Elon Musk]], as an example of [[blackwashing]].<ref name="Nyong'oBacklashResponse" /><ref name="CastingBacklashSalon" /><ref name="CastingBacklashMarySue" /><ref name="CastingBacklashGR" /> Several commentators criticized these responses as racist,<ref name="CastingBacklashGuardian" /><ref name="CastingBacklashDailyNation" /><ref name="CastingBacklashSalon" /><ref name="CastingBacklashMarySue" /> comparing this criticism to similar casting controversies regarding the [[Disney]] film ''[[The Little Mermaid (2023 film)|The Little Mermaid]]'' (2023) and the docudrama ''[[Queen Cleopatra (Netflix)|Queen Cleopatra]]'' (2023).<ref name="CastingBacklashDailyNation" /><ref name="CastingBacklashSalon" /> Tasos Kokkinidis at the ''Greek Reporter'' declared "Nolan's casting ignores the [[Hellenistic period|Hellenistic roots]] of these historical and mythological figures".<ref name="CastingBacklashGR" /> ''[[Salon.com|Salon]]''{{'s}} CK Smith opined the depiction of Helen was up to reinterpretation as a "symbolic character whose meaning has shifted across cultures and eras", arguing the backlash over perceived accuracy was "selective" and focused on social issues regarding representation and identity more than authenticity to the source material and mythology.<ref name="CastingBacklashSalon" /> Meanwhile, Nolan defended Nyong'o's casting, stating that he believed the actress conveyed the "strength and the poise" he found essential for the role. Nyong'o opted to emphasize who Helen was beyond her appearance, stating, "You can't perform beauty". She added the cast was "representative of the world" while "occupying the epic narrative of our time".<ref name="Nyong'oBacklashResponse" /> Writing for ''[[The Guardian]]'', Greek film critic Chris Cotonou called this an "honourable intention", but argued it made the absence of [[Greek diaspora]] representation "even more glaring".<ref name="CastingBacklashGuardian" /> The casting of [[Elliot Page]], a [[transgender man]], was also scrutinized by Musk and other online commenters, who purported speculation that Page was portraying the legendary Greek warrior [[Achilles]]; Page was actually cast as the Greek soldier [[Sinon]].<ref name="NolanBacklashResponse" />

The use of [[American accent]]s and contemporary [[American English]] dialogue from the cast, including British actors Holland and Pattinson, was also criticized.<ref name="AccentsTHR" /><ref name="AccentsSMH" /> [[James Hibberd (writer)|James Hibberd]] of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' called this decision a "striking departure from the [[Queen's Latin|unwritten Hollywood rule]] of characters in [[historical epic]]s employing [[Received Pronunciation|British accents]]".<ref name="AccentsTHR" /> Hibberd and Nell Geraets at ''[[The Sydney Morning Herald]]'' compared this to the British and American accents used in earlier historical epics ''[[The Ten Commandments (1956 film)|The Ten Commandments]]'' (1956), ''[[Ben-Hur (1959 film)|Ben-Hur]]'' (1959), ''[[Spartacus (film)|Spartacus]]'' (1960), and ''[[Gladiator (2000 film)|Gladiator]]'' (2000), as well as in the historical television series ''[[Rome (TV series)|Rome]]'' (2005–2007) and the fantasy series ''[[Game of Thrones]]'' (2011–2019). They contended using regional [[ancient Greek dialects]], such as [[Homeric Greek]], [[Attic Greek]], and [[Hellenistic Koine]], would not be accessible for contemporary audiences,<ref name="AccentsTHR" /><ref name="AccentsSMH" /> contrasting an American accent.<ref name="AccentsMensJournal" /> This change was perceived as anachronistic for an adaptation of ancient [[Greek literature]],<ref name="AccentsTHR" /> with Geraets noting neither British nor American accents were linguistically authentic to the ancient Greek setting,<ref name="AccentsSMH" /> while Hibberd said using American accents "risks sounding a bit silly at times", comparing it to being "more [[Ithaca, New York]], than [[Ithaca, Greece]]". He added that the modern British accent is "traditionally considered universally pleasing and 'just foreign enough' to convey a timeless quality".<ref name="AccentsTHR" /> Nolan insisted on the cast using American accents rather than British or Greek,<ref name="PatelJun2026" /> which ''[[IGN]]''{{'s}} Scott Collura defended, noting that Wilson's translation of the poem similarly translated the text into [[Plain English]], quoting her rationale "Translation always, necessarily, involves interpretation".<ref name="AccentsIGN" /> The accents and [[everyday language]] dialogue were mocked by some online commenters.<ref name="AccentsTHR" /><ref name="AccentsSMH" /><ref name="AccentsIGN" />
