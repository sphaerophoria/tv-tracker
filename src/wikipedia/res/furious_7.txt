'''''Furious 7''''' (also known as <!-- Title elongated as such in the United States, DO NOT CHANGE. -->'''''Fast & Furious 7'''''<!-- Title elongated as such in the United States, DO NOT CHANGE. -->) is a 2015 [[action film]] directed by [[James Wan]] and written by [[Chris Morgan (filmmaker)|Chris Morgan]]. It is the sequel to ''[[Fast & Furious 6]]'' (2013), a follow-up to ''[[The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift]]'' (2006), and the seventh installment in the ''[[Fast & Furious]]'' franchise. The film stars an [[ensemble cast]] including [[Vin Diesel]], [[Paul Walker]] (in his final film role), [[Dwayne Johnson]], [[Michelle Rodriguez]], [[Tyrese Gibson]], [[Ludacris]], [[Jordana Brewster]], [[Djimon Hounsou]], [[Kurt Russell]], and [[Jason Statham]]<!-- PER BILLING BLOCK-->. In the film, [[Dominic Toretto]], [[Brian O'Conner]], and their team are recruited by covert ops leader Mr. Nobody to prevent Mose Jakande (Hounsou), a terrorist, from obtaining a hacking program known as ''God's Eye,'' all while being hunted by Deckard Shaw, the brother of Owen Shaw.

Plans for a seventh installment were first announced in February 2012 when Johnson stated that production on the film would begin after the completion of ''Fast & Furious 6''. In April 2013, Wan, predominantly known for horror films, was announced to direct the film. Casting showed the returns of Diesel and Walker that same month. [[Principal photography]] began that September in [[Atlanta]], but was indefinitely suspended in November after [[Paul Walker#Death|Walker died in a car crash]]; filming resumed in late March 2014 and ended in July, with Walker's brothers Caleb and [[Cody Walker (actor)|Cody]] standing-in to complete his remaining scenes, causing delay to its 2015 release date, with other filming locations including Los Angeles, Colorado, Abu Dhabi and Tokyo. [[Brian Tyler]], who had composed the score for ''[[Fast Five]]'' (2011), returned to compose the seventh installment. With an estimated production budget of up to $250 million, it is [[List of most expensive films|one of the most expensive films ever made]].

''Furious 7'' premiered at the [[Grauman's Chinese Theatre|TCL Chinese Theatre]] in Los Angeles on April 1, 2015, and was released in the United States on April 3, by [[Universal Pictures]]. The film received positive reviews from critics for its performances, action sequences, direction and emotional tribute to Walker, with many considering it to be one of the best films in the franchise. It was a box-office success, grossing $1.515 billion worldwide, making it the <!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved, NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!-->[[2015 in film#Highest-grossing films|third-highest-grossing film of 2015]] and the [[List of highest-grossing films|fourth-<!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!-->highest-grossing film of all time]] at the time of release. It also set a record for the [[List of highest-grossing openings for films|<!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved, NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!-->second-highest<!--This is the PEAK POSITION the film achieved, NOT the CURRENT. Please do NOT adjust!--> opening weekend of its time]]. It was the highest-grossing film of 2015 internationally and became the highest-grossing film of the franchise in the first twelve days of its theatrical release. A sequel titled ''[[The Fate of the Furious]]'' was released on April 14, 2017.

== Plot ==
<!-- Per [[WP:FILMPLOT]], plot summaries should be between 400 to 700 words. Please check the word count before making any additions. -->
After defeating Owen Shaw and securing [[pardon]]s for their past crimes,{{efn|name=FF6|As depicted in ''[[Fast & Furious 6]]'' (2013)}} [[Dominic Toretto]], [[Brian O'Conner]], and the team have returned to the US to live normal lives. Dom tries to help Letty Ortiz regain her memory, while Brian accustoms to life as a father.

Meanwhile, Owen's elder brother [[List of Fast & Furious characters#Deckard Shaw|Deckard Shaw]] vows revenge for the now-comatose Owen. Deckard breaks into the [[Diplomatic Security Service|DSS]] field office in Los Angeles to extract profiles of Dom's crew and fights [[List of Fast & Furious characters#Luke Hobbs|Luke Hobbs]] and [[List of Fast & Furious characters#Elena Neves|Elena Neves]], detonating a bomb that severely injures Hobbs. A [[letter bomb]] sent by Deckard, who has killed Han Lue in Tokyo,{{efn|name=FF6}}{{efn|name=tfatftd|As depicted in ''[[The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift]]'' (2006)}} explodes and destroys the Toretto house.

Dom learns about Deckard from Hobbs and travels to Tokyo to retrieve Han's body. At Han's funeral in LA, Dom spots Deckard and chases him by car, but Deckard flees when a [[covert operation|covert ops]] team, led by government agent Mr. Nobody, arrives. Mr. Nobody tells Dom that he will help them in stopping Deckard if Dom helps him retrieve ''God's Eye'', a computer program capable of tracking anyone in the world on a digital network, and save its creator Ramsey from Mose Jakande, a Nigerian terrorist.

The team, including Dom, Letty, Brian, Tej Parker and Roman Pearce, [[airdrops]] their modified cars over the [[Caucasus Mountains]] in [[Azerbaijan]] and ambush Jakande's convoy, where they rescue Ramsey and leave for the [[Etihad Towers]] in Abu Dhabi. They steal the flash drive containing the God's Eye chip hidden in a [[W Motors Lykan HyperSport]] owned by a billionaire prince. With God's Eye secure, the team uses it to track Deckard, but Deckard, having expected this, has allied with Jakande, who ambushes them. The team is forced to flee while Jakande obtains God's Eye.

The team returns to LA. Dom plans to face Deckard, while Letty, Brian, Tej and Roman resolve to protect Ramsey from Jakande. As Jakande uses God's Eye to doggedly pursue the team with a [[stealth helicopter]] and an [[unmanned combat aerial vehicle|aerial drone]], Ramsey attempts to hack into God's Eye. Hobbs leaves the hospital and destroys the drone while Brian hijacks a [[Cellular repeater|signal repeater tower]] that allows Ramsey to remotely shut down God's Eye.

Jakande attempts to flee and spots Dom and Deckard in a street brawl atop a public parking garage. Jakande turns on Deckard and attacks both men. Dom uses the distraction to defeat Deckard and crashes his [[Dodge Charger (1966)#Second generation|Dodge Charger]] onto Jakande's helicopter, allowing him to leave a bag of grenades on it. Hobbs shoots the grenades, killing Jakande.

Letty tearfully tells an unconscious Dom that she has regained her memories. Dom regains consciousness. Deckard is arrested by Hobbs and locked up in a [[black site]] prison. At the end, Dom and the team acknowledge that Brian has happily retired, opting to be with his family in peace, with another baby on the way. As Dom drives and recalls his memories with Brian, the two bid each other farewell.

===Development===
On October 21, 2011, the ''Los Angeles Times'' reported that [[Universal Pictures|Universal Studios]] was considering filming two sequels—''[[Fast & Furious 6|Fast Six]]'' and ''Fast Seven''—[[Back to back film production|back-to-back]] with a single storyline running through both films. Both would be written by [[Chris Morgan (filmmaker)|Chris Morgan]] and directed by [[Justin Lin]], who had been the franchise's writer and director, respectively, since ''[[The Fast and the Furious: Tokyo Drift]]'' (2006).<ref>{{cite news|last1=Horn |first1=John |last2=Fritz |first2=Ben |url=http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/movies/2011/10/fast-and-furious-6-and-7-speeding-into-production-together.html |title='Fast and Furious' 6 and 7 may speed into production together |work=Los Angeles Times |date=October 21, 2011 |access-date=October 21, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111022130825/http://latimesblogs.latimes.com/movies/2011/10/fast-and-furious-6-and-7-speeding-into-production-together.html |archive-date=October 22, 2011 |url-status=live |df=mdy }}</ref> On December 20, 2011, following the release of ''[[Fast Five]]'' (2011), [[Vin Diesel]] stated that ''[[Fast & Furious 6]]'' (2013) would be split into two parts, with writing for the two films occurring simultaneously.<ref name="SequelDec20">{{cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/vin-diesel-seventh-fast-furious-movie-275800 |title=Vin Diesel Confirms Seventh 'Fast and Furious' Movie in the Works |first=Borys |last=Kit |publisher=Prometheus Global Media |work=The Hollywood Reporter |date=December 20, 2011 |access-date=December 21, 2011 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20111222162636/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/vin-diesel-seventh-fast-furious-movie-275800 |archive-date=December 22, 2011 |url-status=live |df=mdy }}</ref> On the decision, Diesel said:

<blockquote>We have to pay off this story, we have to service all of these character relationships, and when we started mapping all that out it just went beyond 110 pages&nbsp; ... &nbsp;The studio said, 'You can't fit all that story in one damn movie!'<ref name="SequelDec20"/></blockquote>

However, in an interview on February 15, 2012, [[Dwayne Johnson]] stated that the two intended sequels would no longer be filmed simultaneously because of weather issues in filming locations, and that production on this film would only begin after the completion of ''[[Fast & Furious 6]]'' (2013).<ref>{{cite web |first=Terri |last=Schwartz |url=http://moviesblog.mtv.com/2012/02/15/dwayne-johnson-fast-six-fast-seven/ |title=Dwayne Johnson Says 'Fast Six' Begins In May, Won't Shoot Back-To-Back For 'Fast Seven' |publisher=Viacom |work=MTV |date=February 15, 2012 |access-date=February 15, 2012 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20120218012134/http://moviesblog.mtv.com/2012/02/15/dwayne-johnson-fast-six-fast-seven/ |archive-date=February 18, 2012 |url-status=dead |df=mdy }}</ref>

In April 2013, during the post-production of the retitled ''[[Fast & Furious 6]]'' (2013), Lin announced that he would not return to direct the seventh film, as the studio wanted to produce the film on an accelerated schedule for release in summer 2014. This would have required Lin to begin pre-production on the sequel while performing post-production on ''[[Fast & Furious 6]]'' (2013), which he considered would affect the quality of the final product. Despite the usual two-year gap between the previous installments, Universal chose to pursue a sequel quicker due to having fewer reliable franchises than its competitor studios.<ref>{{cite news|first=Borys |last=Kit |url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/justin-lin-wont-direct-fast-432957 |title=Justin Lin Won't Direct 'Fast & Furious 7' (Exclusive) |publisher=Prometheus Global Media |work=The Hollywood Reporter |date=April 4, 2013 |access-date=April 4, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130407035413/http://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/justin-lin-wont-direct-fast-432957 |archive-date=April 7, 2013 |url-status=dead |df=mdy }}</ref> However, subsequent interviews with Lin have suggested that the sixth film was always intended to be the final installment under his direction.<ref>{{cite web|title=Interview: Fast & Furious 6 Director Justin Lin|url=https://www.comingsoon.net/news/movienews.php?id=104306|work=Comingsoon.net|access-date=October 29, 2013|date=May 16, 2013|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003235/https://www.comingsoon.net/movies/features/104306-interview-fast-furious-6-director-justin-lin|url-status=live}}</ref>

In April 2013, Australian director [[James Wan]], predominantly known for [[horror film]]s, was announced as the sequel's director, with [[Neal H. Moritz]] and Michael Fottrell returning to produce and Morgan returning to write the script, his fifth in the franchise. On April 16, 2013, Diesel announced that the sequel would be released on July 11, 2014.<ref name="Sequel">{{cite web|url=https://deadline.com/2013/04/fast-and-furious-7-release-date-july-11-2014-universal-476105/|title='Fast And Furious 7' Gets July 11, 2014 Release Date|website=Deadline Hollywood|date=April 16, 2013|access-date=April 16, 2013|archive-date=April 18, 2013|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130418232714/http://www.deadline.com/2013/04/fast-and-furious-7-release-date-july-11-2014-universal/|url-status=live}}</ref> In May 2013, Diesel said that the sequel would feature Los Angeles, Tokyo, and the Middle East as locations.<ref name="Sequel2">{{cite web|first1=Simon |last1=Reynolds |first2=Ben |last2=Rawson-Jones |url=http://www.digitalspy.co.uk/movies/news/a481826/vin-diesel-fast--furious-7-heading-to-tokyo-middle-east.html |title=Vin Diesel: 'Fast & Furious 7 heading to Tokyo, Middle East' |publisher=[[Hearst Corporation]] |work=[[Digital Spy]] |date=May 16, 2013 |access-date=May 16, 2013 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20130608062047/http://www.digitalspy.co.uk/movies/news/a481826/vin-diesel-fast--furious-7-heading-to-tokyo-middle-east.html |archive-date=June 8, 2013 |url-status=live |df=mdy }}</ref><ref>{{cite news|first=Justin |last=Kroll |url=https://variety.com/2013/scene/vpage/vin-diesel-promises-l-a-setting-for-fast-furious-7-1200486389/ |title=Vin Diesel Promises L.A. ===Stunts===
[[File:Hercules met parachutisten op Ginkelse heide 19 september 2009.jpg|thumb|right|300px|The [[Lockheed C-130 Hercules]]<ref>{{cite news|url=https://edition.cnn.com/2015/04/08/us/c-130-hercules-military-plane-anniversary/|title=The 'Furious 7' plane that just won't quit|first=Thom|last=Patterson|publisher=[[CNN]]|date=April 8, 2015|access-date=April 9, 2015|archive-date=April 13, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150413085751/http://edition.cnn.com/2015/04/08/us/c-130-hercules-military-plane-anniversary|url-status=live}}</ref> was used in the film to carry the vehicles that would drop from 12,000 feet high, above the [[Sonoran Desert]], making cars plummet at a speed of about 130 to 140 miles per hour.<ref>{{cite news|url=https://nypost.com/2015/03/30/how-furious-7-filmmakers-pushed-5-cars-out-a-plane-at-12000-feet/|title=How 'Furious 7' filmmakers pushed 5 cars out of a plane at 12,000 feet|first=Reed|last=Tucker|newspaper=[[New York Post]]|date=March 30, 2015|access-date=April 7, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003238/https://nypost.com/2015/03/30/how-furious-7-filmmakers-pushed-5-cars-out-a-plane-at-12000-feet/|url-status=live}}</ref><ref>{{cite news|url=http://au.ibtimes.com/furious-7-stunt-coordinators-crew-members-speak-about-air-drop-scene-movie-1429693|title='Furious 7' Stunt Coordinators And Crew Members Speak About The Air Drop Scene In The Movie|first=Sachin|last=Trivedi|newspaper=[[International Business Times]]|date=March 16, 2015|access-date=April 7, 2015|archive-date=April 18, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150418112706/http://au.ibtimes.com/furious-7-stunt-coordinators-crew-members-speak-about-air-drop-scene-movie-1429693|url-status=live}}</ref>]]

The "airdrop" sequence was conceived by Razatos, who told ''[[Business Insider]]'' that he wanted to rely more on real stunts rather than [[Computer-generated imagery|CGI]] because he wanted the whole sequence to "feel real" and fulfill audiences' expectations.<ref name="USA"/><ref name="BI">{{cite web|url=http://www.businessinsider.in/How-Furious-7-dropped-real-cars-from-planes-in-its-most-ridiculous-stunt-yet/articleshow/46790017.cms|title=How 'Furious 7' dropped real cars from planes in its most ridiculous stunt yet|first=Jason|last=Guerrasio|website=[[Business Insider]]|date=April 3, 2015|access-date=April 3, 2015|archive-date=April 3, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150403163933/http://www.businessinsider.in/How-Furious-7-dropped-real-cars-from-planes-in-its-most-ridiculous-stunt-yet/articleshow/46790017.cms|url-status=live}}</ref> The stunt took months of prep-solving problems. Cameras needed to be mounted onto cars in a way that they would not be destroyed when the cars landed, and the crew had to figure out a safe way to get the cars out of the plane. They performed a [[dry run (testing)|dry run]] with a single car falling out of a plane<ref name="BI"/> and did this six times.<ref name="USA"/> Cars were dropped from a [[Lockheed C-130 Hercules]] high above the [[Arizona desert]], but close up shots that show the cars landing on a mountain road were filmed in Colorado.<ref name="USA"/> There were two airplanes, flying at a height of 12,000 feet, each dropping two cars apiece.<ref name="BI"/> [[Ballistic Recovery Systems|BRS parachutes]] enabled with GPS were secured to each of the cars before dropping off the C-130 plane. At about 5,000 feet, the parachutes deployed.<ref name="USA"/> Over 10 cameras were used for the sequence. In addition to cameras on the ground, there were cameras remotely operated inside the plane and another three mounted outside each car. Additional cameras were on a helicopter, where Razatos was stationed watching monitors. Three skydivers used in the shoot wore helmet cameras to help shoot the sequence from multiple angles. Sky divers would either jump out before cars or after them.<ref name="BI"/> While all the cars landed on their drop zones, 70% landed perfectly and 30% did not.<ref name="USA"/> For the close-up scenes which show the actors inside their cars, a giant [[gimbal]] with a 360-degree range of movement were attached to each of the cars and was filmed against a [[Chroma key|green screen]] to reproduce their tumble through the sky.<ref name="OCR">{{cite news|url=http://m.ocregister.com/articles/car-656462-cars-furious.html|title=Reel deal steel|first=Susan|last=Carpenter|newspaper=[[Orange County Register]]|access-date=April 10, 2015}}{{Dead link|date=December 2019 |bot=InternetArchiveBot |fix-attempted=yes }}</ref> The last part of the scene, which shows the cars hitting the road was shot separately. To get that right, the team set up a pulley system that had cars six to ten feet above the ground. When they were dropped from the cranes, the stuntmen who were sitting in the driver's seats raced their engines at about 35 to 40 miles per hour and slid to the ground at full speed. Those cranes were then later removed from the film with computers.<ref name="USA"/><ref name="OCR"/> Razatos claims, therefore, that the air drop sequence was "all real" and that it would be "hard to top".<ref name="BI"/><ref name="USA"/>

The scene featuring Brian jumping off a bus off a cliff was performed by a stuntman and was all done without any computer graphics.<ref name="S2">{{cite web|url=http://www.businessinsider.in/One-of-the-most-insane-stunts-in-Furious-7-almost-didn't-happen/articleshow/46818645.cms|title=One of the most insane stunts in 'Furious 7' almost didn't happen|first=Jason|last=Guerrasio|website=[[Business Insider]]|date=April 6, 2015|access-date=April 6, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003234/https://www.businessinsider.in/One-of-the-most-insane-stunts-in-Furious-7-almost-didn%27t-happen/articleshow/46818645.cms|url-status=live}}</ref> The shooting for this particular sequence along with the scene in which Dom and his team are pursuing to rescue Ramsey almost did not happen due to the absence of [[tax break]] in Colorado.<ref name="S2"/> The studio originally wanted to shoot the sequence in Georgia which provides tax breaks for film productions, and then add woods in the background later in post-production to which Razatos denied saying, "the audience is going to know [it's CGI] and aren't going to feel good about it."<ref name="S2"/> Shooting finally took place in Colorado.<ref name="S2"/>

A total of 340 cars were used in the film,<ref name="OCR"/> and more than 230 cars were destroyed in the making of the film, including several black [[Mercedes-Benz]]es, a [[Ford Crown Victoria]], and a [[Mitsubishi Montero]].<ref name="WSJ">{{cite news|url=https://www.wsj.com/articles/furious-7-what-happened-to-the-wrecked-cars-1427907293|title='Furious 7': What Happened to the Wrecked Cars|first=Steve|last=Knopper|newspaper=[[The Wall Street Journal]]|date=April 1, 2015|access-date=April 10, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003239/https://www.wsj.com/articles/furious-7-what-happened-to-the-wrecked-cars-1427907293|url-status=live}}</ref> The film featured the on-screen destruction of a [[W Motors Lykan HyperSport|Lykan HyperSport]] by [[W Motors]], valued at $3.4 million,<ref name="LH">{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/fast-furious-franchise-damage-would-cost-514-million-expert-estimates-990021|title='Fast & Furious' Franchise Damage Would Cost $514 Million, Expert Estimates|author=Ashley Lee|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=March 30, 2017|access-date=March 31, 2017|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003254/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/heat-vision/justice-league-zack-snyder-brings-banter-heavy-footage-cinemacon-989853|url-status=live}}</ref> though the actual car destroyed was a less expensive model made for the film rather than one of the seven actual production HyperSports.<ref>[https://www.theverge.com/2015/3/24/8282803/furious-7-car-coordinator-interview A Q&A with Dennis McCarthy, the man behind Furious 7's insane cast of cars] {{Webarchive|url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190404044048/https://www.theverge.com/2015/3/24/8282803/furious-7-car-coordinator-interview |date=April 4, 2019 }}. ''[[The Verge]]'', March 24, 2015.</ref> The mountain-highway chase scene on Colorado's [[Monarch Pass]] proved to be the most damaging sequence with over 40 vehicles being destroyed.<ref name="WSJ"/><ref>{{cite news|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/furious-7-230-cars-were-787486|title='Furious 7': Over 230 Cars Were Destroyed During Film's Shoot|first=Ryan|last=Gajewski|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=April 9, 2015|access-date=April 10, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003242/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/furious-7-230-cars-were-787486|url-status=live}}</ref> Only 10 percent of the action sequences in the film were computer-generated, and even then, much of the CGI was employed simply to erase the wires and other contraptions that were used to film real cars and drivers or to add a background.<ref name="OCR"/> It took more than 3,500 [[Man-hour|man-days]] to complete the various stunts of the film.<ref name="OCR"/>

===Redevelopment of Walker's character===
{{quote box|width=33%|align=right|quote=When I first heard the news, I was shell-shocked like everyone, and it took me days to come to terms with it. And then after that, heartbreaks started sinking in and we realized that Paul [Walker] wasn't going to be around with us anymore moving forward, and it was a really hard one. And finishing the movie was the last thing on my mind at that point. It was more the idea of ... picking up the pieces, going back on set, rallying the team, the cast, and the crew, and as the director, having to put on the brave face and champion and push everyone along. The idea of that was very daunting for me, but it became very apparent to all of us that we needed to finish this movie to honour Paul's legacy and to basically honour his memories ... it was about making this movie for Paul.|source= —[[James Wan]], director of ''Furious 7''<ref>{{cite web|url=http://m.hindustantimes.com/hollywood/before-paul-walker-died-this-was-the-fast-and-furious-7-climax/article1-1335118.aspx|title=Before Paul Walker died, this was the Fast and Furious 7 climax|work=[[Hindustan Times]]|date=April 8, 2015|access-date=April 9, 2015|archive-date=May 23, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150523000727/http://www.hindustantimes.com/hollywood/before-paul-walker-died-this-was-the-fast-and-furious-7-climax/article1-1335118.aspx|url-status=dead}}</ref>}}

In January 2014, ''[[Time (magazine)|Time]]'' reported that Walker's character, Brian O'Conner, would be retired instead of killed, and that new scenes would be developed in order to allow the franchise to continue without him.<ref>{{cite magazine |url=https://entertainment.time.com/2014/01/06/heres-what-will-happen-to-paul-walkers-character-in-fast-and-furious-7/ |title=Paul Walker's Fast and Furious Fate: How The Movie Will Deal With His Death |last=Stampler |first=Laura |magazine=[[Time (magazine)|Time]] |date=January 6, 2014 |access-date=January 13, 2014 |archive-date=January 10, 2014 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20140110150856/http://entertainment.time.com/2014/01/06/heres-what-will-happen-to-paul-walkers-character-in-fast-and-furious-7/ |url-status=live }}</ref> To recreate Walker's likeness, the filmmakers hired [[Peter Jackson]]'s [[Weta Digital]] visual effects company (which had previously produced the imagery of [[Gollum]] in [[The Lord of the Rings (film series)|''The Lord of the Rings'' franchise]] and [[Caesar (Planet of the Apes)|Caesar]] in the [[Planet of the Apes|''Planet of the Apes'' franchise]]).<ref name="Giardina">{{cite news|last1=Giardina|first1=Carolyn|title=How 'Furious 7' Brought the Late Paul Walker Back to Life|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/behind-screen/how-furious-7-brought-late-845763|access-date=6 January 2017|work=The Hollywood Reporter|publisher=Prometheus Global Media|date=11 December 2015|location=Los Angeles|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003243/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/behind-screen/how-furious-7-brought-late-845763|url-status=live}}</ref> Initially, what Weta could do was severely constrained by the quality of the reference materials available for Walker's physical appearance. In April 2014, it was reported that Walker's brothers Caleb and [[Cody Walker (actor)|Cody]] had been hired as stand-ins.<ref>{{cite web |url=https://deadline.com/2014/04/paul-walker-brothers-fast-furious-7-filming-help-715202/ |title=Paul Walker's Brothers Jumping In To Help Finish 'Fast & Furious 7' Action Scenes |website=[[Deadline Hollywood]] |date=April 15, 2014 |access-date=May 4, 2014 |archive-date=December 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003246/https://deadline.com/2014/04/paul-walker-brothers-fast-furious-7-filming-help-715202/ |url-status=live }}</ref> Their strong resemblance to their late brother meant the filmmakers could use scans of their bodies instead of recreating Walker's entire body from scratch. The final film showed Walker's face superimposed over the bodies of his brothers or actor [[John Brotherton]] in 350 visual effects shots. 260 used a computer-generated face, while 90 repurposed actual footage of Walker's face borrowed from outtakes or older footage.<ref name="Giardina"/><ref name="Polygon">{{Cite web |url=https://www.polygon.com/2015/10/20/9577863/furious-7-used-350-cgi-shots-of-paul-walker |title=Furious 7 used 350 CGI shots of Paul Walker |website=[[Polygon (website)|Polygon]] |date=October 20, 2015 |access-date=May 17, 2020 |archive-date=December 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003246/https://www.polygon.com/2015/10/20/9577863/furious-7-used-350-cgi-shots-of-paul-walker |url-status=live }}</ref><ref>{{Cite web |url=https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a674223/furious-7-effects-team-open-up-about-creating-un-filmed-paul-walker-scenes/ |title=How CGI saved Fast 7's Paul Walker scenes |website=[[Digital Spy]] |date=October 17, 2015 |access-date=May 17, 2020 |archive-date=December 4, 2020 |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003245/https://www.digitalspy.com/movies/a674223/furious-7-effects-team-open-up-about-creating-un-filmed-paul-walker-scenes/ |url-status=live }}</ref>

===Visual effects and animation===
[[Weta Digital]], [[Digital Domain]], [[Eyeline|Scanline VFX]], the [[Moving Picture Company]] (MPC), and [[Rodeo FX]] provided the visual effects and animation for the film.<ref name="Weta">{{Cite web |title=''Furious 7'' |url=https://www.wetafx.co.nz/films/filmography/furious-7 |website=[[Wētā FX]] |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref><ref name="Domain">{{Cite web |title=''Furious 7'' {{!}} Features {{!}} Digital Domain |url=https://digitaldomain.com/work/furious-7/ |website=[[Digital Domain]] |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref><ref name="ArtOfVFX">{{Cite web |title=''Fast & Furious 7'' |url=https://www.artofvfx.com/fast-furious-7/ |website=The Art of VFX |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref><ref name="MPC">{{Cite web |title=''Furious 7'' |url=https://www.mpcvfx.com/en/filmography/furious-7/ |website=[[Moving Picture Company]] |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref><ref name="Rodeo">{{Cite web |title=''Furious 7'' |url=https://www.rodeofx.com/project/furious-7 |website=[[Rodeo FX]] |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref>

Weta Digital's major role in the visual effects production was for the reconstruction of Paul Walker's character, Brian O'Conner. Weta's visual effects house completed over 350 shots with visual effects, with a majority of the shots using repurposed footage of Walker's face. [[Joe Letteri]] and [[Martin Hill (visual effects artist)|Martin Hill]] served as the [[Visual effects supervisor|visual effects supervisors]] for Weta, while Daniel Barrett served as the [[animation supervisor]].<ref name="Giardina"/><ref name="Polygon"/> In an interview with ''[[Fxguide]]'', Letteri said, "We tried to keep as true to Paul as we could, in whatever way we could."<ref>{{Cite web |title=''Furious 7''{{'}}s most stunning effect: Paul Walker |url=https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/furious-7s-most-stunning-effect-paul-walker/ |website=[[Fxguide]] |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref> Jay Barton led Digital Domain's visual effects team as the visual effects supervisor, and the studio produced over 800 shots with a team of close to 300 artists; their staff was split between Los Angeles and [[Vancouver]] for this process.<ref name="DigDomVariety">{{Cite web |last1=Gray |first1=Tim |date=October 15, 2015 |title=Paul Walker Furious 7 VFX |url=https://variety.com/2015/film/awards/furious-7-visual-effects-paul-walker-1201618224/ |website=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]] |access-date=November 26, 2025}}</ref> Jon Cowley and Danielle Plantec led Scanline as the visual effects supervisors at the main studio in [[Munich]], while Mark Curtis served as the visual effects supervisor, and Laura Schultz served as the visual effects producer, at the Moving Picture Company (MPC)'s Canadian studio in Vancouver.<ref name="MPC"/>

MPC handled the visual effects of the film's final pursuit sequence in Los Angeles, where all of the cars were digitally recreated with computer-generated imagery for fully CGI shots, along with over a hundred in-car blue screen composite shots, which were done by CGI supervisor [[Bryan Litson]]; a large portion of MPC's workforce handled the visual effects for the drone, with director James Wan saying he wanted to "create an enigmatic and malevolent character that would provide a constant but elusive threat to our heroes." Inspiration for the drone were drawn by [[United States Armed Forces|US Military]]'s [[Northrop Grumman RQ-4 Global Hawk|Global Hawk Drone]], an [[Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II|A-10 Warthog]], and [[World War II]]-era Corsair and Helldivers.<ref name="MPC"/>

Rodeo FX visual effects supervisor Laurent Spillemaecker, visual effects producer Audrey Boivin, CGI supervisor Sébastien Francoeur, and compositing supervisor Xavier Fourmond—handled the visual effects for two fight sequences in the film: the first being the office fight sequence between Statham and Johnson, with Rodeo using "set extensions and CGI to add flying papers, fire, smoke and explosions," while also adding in "a CGI double for The Rock who's falling from the window"; the CGI double for Johnson was one of many required for the sequence, and the second being the car collision sequence between Diesel and Statham; the studio produced the visual effect of the shattering of the glass coming from the colliding cars.<ref name="Rodeo"/>

===Music===

The [[film score|musical score]] was composed by [[Brian Tyler]], who scored the third, fourth and fifth installments of the series.<ref name="FilmMusicReporter">{{cite web|title=Brian Tyler to Score 'Fast & Furious 7'|url=http://filmmusicreporter.com/2013/10/22/brian-tyler-to-score-fast-furious-7/|work=Film Music Reporter|access-date=October 23, 2013|date=October 22, 2013|archive-date=March 16, 2016|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20160316023913/http://filmmusicreporter.com/2013/10/22/brian-tyler-to-score-fast-furious-7/|url-status=live}}</ref> "There's an emotional component to ''Fast & Furious 7'' that is unique", said Tyler about his experience scoring. "I think people are really going to be amazed by it."<ref>{{cite web|last1=Niles|first1=Jon|title=[Exclusive Interview]: Brian Tyler - Composer For 'The Expendables 3,' 'TMNT,' 'Avengers: Age of Ultron,' & Many More Movies Talks Music To Film, TV & Video Games|url=http://www.musictimes.com/articles/8767/20140814/exclusive-interview-brian-tyler-composer-expendables-3-tmnt-avengers-age.htm|website=Music Times|access-date=March 16, 2015|date=August 14, 2014|archive-date=May 12, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150512015101/http://www.musictimes.com/articles/8767/20140814/exclusive-interview-brian-tyler-composer-expendables-3-tmnt-avengers-age.htm|url-status=live}}</ref> A soundtrack album to the film was released by [[Atlantic Records]] on March 17, 2015,<ref>{{cite magazine|last1=Gallo|first1=Phil|title='Furious 7' Soundtrack Launches With 7 Singles|url=https://www.billboard.com/articles/6472998/furious-7-soundtrack-listing|magazine=[[Billboard (magazine)|Billboard]]|access-date=March 16, 2015|date=February 17, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003247/https://www.billboard.com/articles/6472998/furious-7-soundtrack-listing|url-status=live}}</ref> while Tyler's score was released by [[Back Lot Music]] on March 31.

When discussing the creation of the score, Tyler explained: "It was a pleasure to collaborate with [[James Wan|James]] on ''Furious 7'', as he wanted the emotion of the themes to be the primary focus. The music uses modern recording techniques, vintage modular synthesizers, mashed-up beats, drums and tweaked remix elements along with classic film scoring traditions including full orchestra, piano, voice and classical guitar. I am so proud of the movie, and I dedicate the score to the memory of [[Paul Walker]] and all the joy he brought."

In describing Tyler's score, Wan remarked, "Brian's amazing score gave this movie life. He did an incredible job of crafting an electrifying score for the bombastic action moments, one that is balanced by the beautiful and emotional themes of the characters that underline the heart of this movie."<ref>{{cite press release |url=http://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/furious-7-original-motion-picture-score-album-to-be-released-on-back-lot-music-march-31st-featuring-new-music-by-composer-brian-tyler-300056368.html|title="FURIOUS 7" Original Motion Picture Score Album to Be Released on Back Lot Music March 31st Featuring New Music by Composer Brian Tyler|publisher=PR Newswire.com |accessdate=2015-03-26}}</ref>

===Critical response===
''Furious 7'' received positive reviews from critics for its poignant tribute to Walker.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Kelley|first1=Sonaiya|title=''Furious 7'' opens to overwhelmingly positive reviews from critics|url=http://mashable.com/2015/04/04/critics-reactions-furious-7/#tkRuHDbjWmqY|access-date=November 26, 2017|work=[[Mashable]]|date=April 5, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003346/https://mashable.com/2015/04/04/critics-reactions-furious-7/#tkRuHDbjWmqY|url-status=live}}</ref> The [[review aggregator]] website [[Rotten Tomatoes]] reported an approval rating of 81% with an average score of 7.70/10, based on 279 reviews, the highest-rated film in the franchise to date. The website's critical consensus reads, "Serving up a fresh round of over-the-top thrills while adding unexpected dramatic heft, ''Furious 7'' keeps the franchise moving in more ways than one."<ref>{{cite Rotten Tomatoes|id=furious_7|type=m|title=Furious 7|access-date=March 3, 2026|archive-date=May 8, 2015|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20150508223046/http://www.rottentomatoes.com/m/furious_7|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Metacritic]], which uses a weighted average, assigned the film a score of 67 out of 100 based on 50 critics, indicating "generally favorable reviews". It is the highest-rated ''Fast & Furious'' film on both websites.<ref>{{cite Metacritic|id=furious-7|title=Furious 7|type=movie|access-date=January 6, 2021|archive-date=March 27, 2019|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20190327030251/https://www.metacritic.com/movie/furious-7|url-status=live}}</ref> Audiences polled by [[CinemaScore]] gave the film an average grade of "A" on an A+ to F scale.<ref>{{cite news|last1=Lang|first1=Brent|title=Box Office: 'Furious 7' Destroys Records With $143.6 Million Debut|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/news/furious-7-box-office-debut-1201466584/|access-date=December 3, 2017|work=Variety|agency=Penske Media Corporation|date=April 5, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003348/https://variety.com/2015/film/news/furious-7-box-office-debut-1201466584/|url-status=live}}</ref>

The film received highly positive reviews upon its secret screening at the [[South by Southwest]] film festival on March 16, 2015. Ramin Setoodeh of ''[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]'' noted that fans began lining up outside four hours before the film was scheduled to start. The film closed with a tribute to Walker, which left many in the theater "holding back tears".<ref>{{cite magazine|url=https://variety.com/2015/film/news/furious-7-premieres-at-sxsw-to-cheers-for-paul-walker-1201453405/|title='Furious 7′ Premieres at SXSW to Cheers for Paul Walker|first=Ramin|last=Setoodah|magazine=[[Variety (magazine)|Variety]]|date=March 16, 2015|access-date=March 16, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003346/https://variety.com/2015/film/news/furious-7-premieres-at-sxsw-to-cheers-for-paul-walker-1201453405/|url-status=live}}</ref> [[Wesley Morris]] wrote, "Who would have thought that a series addicted to the high of movement could also summon a solemnity that leaves you moved?"<ref>{{Cite web |last=Morris |first=Wesley |date=April 3, 2015 |title=» Drivin' and Cryin': Old Ridiculousness and New Emotion Converge in 'Furious 7' |url=https://grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/furious-7-review/ |url-status=live |archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20200925082113/http://grantland.com/hollywood-prospectus/furious-7-review/ |archive-date=2020-09-25 |access-date=2022-08-15 |website=[[Grantland]] |language=en-US}}</ref> [[A. O. Scott]] of ''[[The New York Times]]'' said, "''Furious 7'' extends its predecessors' inclusive, stereotype-resistant ethic. Compared to almost any other large-scale, big-studio enterprise, the ''Furious'' brand practices a slick, no-big-deal multiculturalism, and nods to both feminism and domestic traditionalism."<ref>{{cite news|url=https://nytimes.com/2015/04/03/movies/review-in-furious-7-a-franchise-continues-to-roar.html?referrer=&_r=0|title=Review: In 'Furious 7,' a Franchise Continues to Roar|author=A.O. Scott|newspaper=[[The New York Times]]|date=April 1, 2015|access-date=April 5, 2015|archive-date=December 4, 2020|archive-url=https://web.archive.org/web/20201204003359/https://www.nytimes.com/2015/04/03/movies/review-in-furious-7-a-franchise-continues-to-roar.html?referrer=&_r=0|url-status=live|url-access=limited}}</ref> John DeFore of ''[[The Hollywood Reporter]]'' criticized the film however, describing it as "stupidly diverting", saying the running time was "overinflated"; he compared watching the film to a morbid game, in addition to criticizing the screenplay.<ref name="THRreview">{{cite web|url=https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-reviews/furious-7-film-review-sxsw-781615/|title='Furious 7': SXSW Review|first=John|last=DeFore|work=[[The Hollywood Reporter]]|date=March 16, 2015|access-date=March 16, 2015}}</ref>
